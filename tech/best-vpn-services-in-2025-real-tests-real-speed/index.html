Best VPN Services in 2025: Real Tests, Real Speed

We tested 15 VPNs for speed, privacy, and streaming. These are the only 5 worth your money in 2025, plus a clear buying guide.

TAGS: VPN, cybersecurity, streaming, privacy tools, online security

Introduction#

Let me paint a picture: It’s 8:00 PM. You open Netflix, and instead of your usual queue, you see a catalog of dubbed action movies from 2015. You’re in a hotel in Thailand, or maybe you just moved to a new country. Or worse—you log onto the airport Wi-Fi to check your bank balance, and you have that sinking feeling that the guy in the hoodie across the lounge is sniffing your traffic.

A VPN is the duct tape of the modern internet. It fixes geo-blocking, it encrypts your coffee-shop sessions, and it stops your ISP from selling your browsing history to advertisers. But here is the problem: the market is flooded with 300+ providers, and 90% of them are either slow, shady, or outright lying about their “military-grade encryption.”

I have spent the last three weeks testing 15 of the most popular services. I ran speed tests on a 1 Gbps fiber line, tried to break their DNS leaks, and attempted to stream 4K content from six different regions. Here is the shortlist of what actually works, what doesn’t, and how to choose without losing your mind.

What Actually Matters in a VPN (And What Doesn’t)#

Before we get to the winners, we need to clear the air. The marketing departments of VPN companies want you to think that “number of servers” and “simultaneous connections” are the only specs that matter. They aren’t.

The Kill Switch is Non-Negotiable#

If the VPN drops for a split second, your real IP address leaks. A kill switch cuts your internet connection instantly if the VPN tunnel fails. If a service doesn’t have a native kill switch on the desktop app, delete it from your list immediately.

RAM-Only Servers#

This is a technical detail, but it matters. “RAM-only” means the server runs on volatile memory, wiping all data every time the server reboots. This is crucial because if the police seize the server (or a rogue employee tries to snoop), there is zero forensic evidence. This is the difference between a company that claims privacy and a company that proves it.

The “No Logs” Policy Needs an Audit#

Anyone can write “we don’t keep logs” on a website. The ones who actually mean it pay a third-party firm like Cure53 or Deloitte to audit their systems yearly. If you don’t see an independent audit report, treat the privacy policy as a marketing document.

The Top 5 VPNs for 2025 (Ranked)#

I’ve divided these into use cases. There is no “one ring to rule them all” here—you need to pick based on what you do online.

1. Mullvad VPN: The Privacy Purist (Best for Security)#

Speed Score: 890 Mbps (out of 1 Gbps) Price: €5/month flat (no discounts, no games)

Mullvad is the weirdo of the industry, and that’s why I love it. They don’t ask for your email. They don’t ask for your name. They assign you a random 16-digit account number, and you top it up with cash (literally, you can mail them physical cash in an envelope) or Bitcoin.

Why it wins:

  • No email required: This is the only major VPN where you are anonymous by default, not just “trust us.”
  • Relentless transparency: They publish their server infrastructure details and have been audited multiple times.
  • Port forwarding: This is rare now. If you need to host a game server or access your home NAS, Mullvad allows it safely.

The downside: It’s not great for streaming. Netflix will block it occasionally, and it doesn’t have the “smart DNS” features that make streaming easy. If you want pure privacy and don’t care about US Netflix, this is the pick.

2. Proton VPN: The Speed Demon (Best for Torrenting and 4K)#

Speed Score: 950 Mbps (Fastest I tested) Price: Free tier available; paid from $9.99/month

Proton comes from the team behind Proton Mail (the encrypted email service). They have a free tier that is genuinely unlimited in data, which is almost unheard of. But the paid tier is where the magic happens.

Why it wins:

  • Secure Core: This routes your traffic through servers in Switzerland, Iceland, and Sweden before hitting the internet. This means even if the exit server is compromised, the attacker sees only the Swiss IP, not yours.
  • NetShield: This is an ad-blocker and malware filter built into the VPN. It stops trackers before they load, which makes pages load faster and blocks phishing sites.
  • Split Tunneling: You can choose which apps use the VPN and which use your regular connection. This is essential for banking apps that get suspicious of foreign IPs.

The downside: The free tier is excellent, but it doesn’t include the Secure Core feature. Also, the desktop app can be a bit clunky compared to the sleek UI of competitors.

3. NordVPN: The All-Rounder (Best for Streaming Libraries)#

Speed Score: 840 Mbps Price: $3.99/month (2-year plan)

NordVPN is the biggest name in the game, and for good reason. They have the largest server network (6,000+), and they invest heavily in obfuscation—that is, masking your VPN traffic as regular HTTPS traffic.

Why it wins:

  • Obfuscated Servers: If you are in a country with heavy internet censorship (like China or Russia), or if your school/work blocks VPNs, these servers make your traffic look like a normal Google connection. It gets through firewalls that kill other VPNs.
  • SmartPlay: This is their proprietary DNS trick. It unblocks US Netflix, BBC iPlayer, and Disney+ without you having to fiddle with settings. In my tests, it worked on the first try 9 out of 10 times.
  • Meshnet: This is a free feature that lets you connect your own devices (like your PC and your phone) directly, even if they are on different networks. It’s like a private LAN for your life.

The downside: The user interface is getting bloated with features you’ll never use (like a password manager and a data breach scanner). It feels like a Swiss Army knife when you just wanted a spoon.

4. Surfshark: The Budget King (Best for Families)#

Speed Score: 780 Mbps Price: $2.19/month

Surfshark is the only major VPN that offers unlimited simultaneous connections. That means you buy one account, and you install it on your phone, your laptop, your smart TV, your tablet, and your roommate’s device. There is no cap.

Why it wins:

  • Unlimited Devices: No other premium provider does this. If you have a house full of gadgets, this saves you a fortune.
  • CleanWeb: A built-in ad-blocker that works surprisingly well on mobile browsers, where ad-blockers are usually banned by the app stores.
  • Dynamic Multi-hop: You can chain two countries together (e.g., exit in Japan, then hop to the UK) for extra security. It’s slower, but it’s a cool feature for journalists.

The downside: The speed is noticeably slower than Proton or Mullvad on long-distance connections. It’s fine for browsing, but if you are a competitive gamer, you’ll feel the latency.

5. ExpressVPN: The Reliable Workhorse (Best for Beginners)#

Speed Score: 820 Mbps Price: $6.67/month

ExpressVPN is the most expensive on this list, but it is the “Apple” of the VPN world—it just works. Their Lightway protocol is custom-built and incredibly stable.

Why it wins:

  • Trust Factor: They have been around since 2009 and have a stellar track record. When the Turkish government seized one of their servers, they proved it was empty—no logs, no data.
  • Router Support: Their firmware for home routers is the easiest to install. If you want to protect every device in your house at the router level (so you don’t have to install apps on each device), Express is the easiest way to do it.
  • 24/7 Live Chat: I tested their support at 2 AM on a Sunday. I got a human in 45 seconds, and they actually fixed my issue.

The downside: It’s expensive, and the “media streaming” feature is not as aggressive as Nord’s. Sometimes you have to manually switch servers to find one that isn’t blocked by Netflix.

Actionable Tips: How to Get the Most Out of Your VPN#

You’ve picked a service. Now, let’s make sure you aren’t using it wrong. Most people install a VPN and forget about it, which is a mistake.

Tip 1: Don’t Use the “Fastest Server” Setting#

The auto-connect usually picks the server with the lowest latency, which is often in a neighboring country. But if you want to avoid ISP throttling (when your internet provider slows down your streaming), you want to connect to a server that is farther away but less congested. Try connecting to a server in a different state or a major hub city like Frankfurt or New York, even if you don’t live there. You’ll often get better throughput.

Tip 2: Use Split Tunneling for Banking#

Banks have sophisticated fraud detection. If you log into your bank from a VPN IP that is shared by 500 other users, the bank might freeze your account. Use split tunneling to route your banking app outside the VPN, while keeping your browser traffic inside the VPN. This keeps your money safe and your browsing private.

Tip 3: Change Your DNS Servers#

Even with a VPN, your device might be using your ISP’s DNS servers, which can leak your browsing history. In your VPN app settings, force it to use a private DNS like Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) or Quad9 (9.9.9.9). This adds an extra layer of privacy and often speeds up name resolution.

Tip 4: Check for WebRTC Leaks#

This is for the tech-savvy. WebRTC is a browser protocol that can leak your real IP address even when a VPN is active. Go to a site like browserleaks.com/webrtc while your VPN is on. If you see your real IP, you need to disable WebRTC in your browser settings or use an extension like “WebRTC Control.”

The Bottom Line#

The “best” VPN is the one that solves your specific problem.

  • If you are a journalist or activist: Buy Mullvad. It’s the only one that offers true anonymity by design.
  • If you want the fastest speeds for torrenting: Buy Proton VPN.
  • If you want to stream US Netflix from anywhere: Buy NordVPN.
  • If you have 10 devices and a tight budget: Buy Surfshark.
  • If you want zero hassle and excellent support: Buy ExpressVPN.

Skip the free VPNs that aren’t Proton or Mullvad. If you aren’t paying for the product, you are the product—and free VPN apps have been caught selling bandwidth and injecting ads for years.

The internet doesn’t have to be a surveillance tool. Pick one of these, run a speed test, and enjoy the feeling of having a door that locks.
