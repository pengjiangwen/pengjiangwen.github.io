Best Budget Laptops 2024: Top Picks Under $700

We tested the best budget laptops of 2024. From Chromebooks to Windows machines, here are the top picks for students, work, and everyday use.

TITLE: Best Budget Laptops 2024: Top Picks Under $700

DESCRIPTION: We tested the best budget laptops of 2024. From Chromebooks to Windows machines, here are the top picks for students, work, and everyday use.

TAGS: budget laptops, best laptops 2024, cheap laptops, laptops under 700, student laptops

Introduction#

Let’s be honest: the laptop market in 2024 is a minefield. You walk into a big-box store, see a shiny silver machine for $299, and think you’ve scored a deal. Then you get home, open 14 browser tabs, and the fan sounds like a jet engine taking off. I’ve been there. I’ve bought the $250 doorbuster that couldn’t handle a Zoom call without melting.

Here is the good news: the “budget” category has improved dramatically over the last two years. The rise of ARM-based processors in Chromebooks and the aggressive pricing of AMD’s Ryzen 7000 series have pushed performance down to price points we only dreamed of in 2020. You no longer need to spend $1,000 to get a machine that feels snappy.

But you still need to know what to look for. Specifically, you need to avoid anything with an Intel Celeron or Pentium processor from before 2022. Those are e-waste. This guide is based on hands-on testing and hours of spec-sheet analysis. I’m going to show you the specific models that are worth your money right now, and why they beat the competition.

The Golden Rule: Don’t Buy Based on Storage Size#

Before we dive into the specific picks, we need to talk about the biggest trap in the budget aisle: the 1TB HDD.

You will see laptops with “1TB Storage” for $450. They are using a spinning hard drive (HDD). This is a 20-year-old technology. It makes your computer feel slow before you even open an app. In 2024, you absolutely require an SSD (Solid State Drive) . An SSD makes booting up take 10 seconds instead of 2 minutes. It makes apps open instantly.

If a laptop has an HDD, walk away. Even if it has a great processor, the HDD will bottleneck everything. Aim for at least 256GB SSD. If you need more space, buy an external drive later—it’s cheaper and faster than paying a premium for a massive internal drive at this price point.

The Best Overall: Acer Aspire 5 (2024 Model)#

Price: ~$499 - $549

If you want a traditional Windows laptop that does everything reasonably well, the Acer Aspire 5 is still the undisputed king of the budget category. But you have to be careful with the spec sheet.

The 2024 model finally ditched the older Ryzen 5 5500U in favor of the Ryzen 5 7530U. This is a 6-core, 12-thread processor that is honestly overkill for most students. It handles heavy multitasking—think 20 Chrome tabs, Spotify, and a word processor—without breaking a sweat.

Why it wins:

  • Upgradeable RAM: This is rare in budget laptops. You can pop off the bottom panel and upgrade from 8GB to 16GB later. This extends the life of the laptop by years.
  • Port Selection: It has USB-C, USB-A, HDMI, and an Ethernet port. You don’t need a dongle for a projector or a wired network connection.
  • Keyboard: It has a full number pad. For anyone in accounting or data entry, this is a lifesaver.

The catch: The screen is only 1080p, and the color accuracy is mediocre. It’s bright enough for indoor use, but you won’t want to edit photos on it. Also, the battery life is average—around 7 hours of mixed use. If you need all-day battery, check the Chromebook below.

Best for Students: Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 3 Chromebook (14")#

Price: ~$349

Here is where I change my tune. I used to hate Chromebooks. I thought they were useless if you weren’t online. That changed with the introduction of the Snapdragon processor in Chromebooks, but more specifically, the Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 3 with the MediaTek Kompanio 520 chip.

This is the laptop I recommend to high schoolers and college freshmen who live in Google Workspace (Docs, Sheets, Gmail). Why? Because it does 90% of what a student needs for a fraction of the price, and the battery lasts an entire school day.

Why it wins:

  • Battery Life: I’m talking 12+ hours of real-world use. You can leave the charger at home.
  • Fanless Design: It is completely silent. No whirring fans in a quiet library.
  • The Screen: It’s a 1080p IPS panel. At this price, many Chromebooks use terrible TN panels that look washed out. This one is actually vibrant and clear.

The catch: You are limited to the Chrome browser and Android apps. If your major requires specific Windows software (like AutoCAD or SPSS), you cannot use this. Also, you need to be comfortable with cloud storage; the internal 64GB is just enough for apps and cache.

Actionable Tip: If you buy this, get the 8GB RAM version. The 4GB version is $30 cheaper, but it will struggle with heavy tabs. The extra $30 is the best value upgrade in tech right now.

Best for Creatives on a Budget: ASUS Vivobook 16X#

Price: ~$649

This is the sleeper hit of 2024. Most budget laptops skip on the display to save money. The ASUS Vivobook 16X does not. It packs a 16-inch 16:10 display with a 120Hz refresh rate.

For anyone doing photo editing or just consuming media, this is a game-changer. The 16:10 aspect ratio gives you more vertical space, which means less scrolling in documents and spreadsheets. The 120Hz refresh rate makes scrolling feel buttery smooth—something you usually only get on $1,500 gaming laptops.

Why it wins:

  • The Display: 16" WUXGA (1920x1200), 120Hz, 100% sRGB. This is the best screen you can get under $700, period.
  • Performance: It comes with a Ryzen 5 7520U (4 cores) or Ryzen 7 (if you catch a sale). It handles Photoshop and Lightroom classic surprisingly well.
  • Build Quality: The chassis feels sturdier than the Acer. It has a metal lid, which adds a premium feel.

The catch: The battery life is poor. With that high refresh rate screen, you’re looking at 5-6 hours max. Also, the trackpad is a bit stiff. I recommend carrying a wireless mouse if you buy this, as the click mechanism requires more pressure than I like.

The Budget Gaming Pick: HP Victus 15#

Price: ~$699 (often on sale)

I know, I know. “Gaming” and “budget” don’t usually mix. But the HP Victus 15 is the exception. In late 2024, you can frequently find this with an RTX 2050 or RTX 3050 GPU for under $700. This is the cheapest way to get a dedicated graphics card.

Why does this matter for non-gamers? Because a dedicated GPU helps with video editing, 3D rendering, and even just smooth desktop rendering on 4K monitors. But if you do game, this will run Fortnite, Valorant, and CS2 at 60fps+ easily.

Why it wins:

  • The GPU: The RTX 3050 (4GB VRAM) is old, but it still smokes integrated graphics. It allows for CUDA acceleration in Adobe Premiere Pro, which speeds up video exports by 3x compared to a CPU-only laptop.
  • Upgradeability: Two M.2 SSD slots and two RAM slots. This is a tinkerer’s dream.

The catch: It is heavy (over 5 lbs) and the plastic build is creaky. The screen is 144Hz, but the color accuracy is average. Also, the battery life is terrible—expect 4 hours. This is a desktop replacement, not a portable machine.

My Tip: Wait for a sale on this one. It frequently drops to $599 on Amazon or Best Buy during holiday weekends. Don’t pay full price.

Don’t Forget the Mac Option: MacBook Air M1#

Price: ~$649 (refurbished/clearance)

I know this is a “budget” article, but I’d be doing you a disservice not to mention the elephant in the room. The MacBook Air M1 (2020) is still being sold by retailers at a steep discount to clear inventory. You can find it new for around $750, but refurbished units from Apple are around $649.

This is the most durable, efficient laptop ever made. The M1 chip is still faster than 90% of the Windows laptops in this price range. The build quality is aluminum, the trackpad is the best in the industry, and the battery lasts 15 hours.

Why it wins:

  • Resale Value: In 3 years, a MacBook Air M1 will still sell for $300. A $500 Windows laptop will be worth $100.
  • Display: The Retina display (2560x1600) is sharper and more color-accurate than any Windows laptop under $800.

The catch: It only has 8GB of RAM (which is fine for most, but not heavy multitaskers). It has two USB-C ports (you need a dongle for HDMI). And macOS is an adjustment if you’ve been on Windows your whole life.

The Verdict: Which Should You Buy?#

To make this easy, here’s the quick decision tree:

  1. If you need Windows and want to upgrade later: Get the Acer Aspire 5. It’s the safe, smart choice.
  2. If you live in Google Chrome and need battery life: Get the Lenovo IdeaPad Slim 3 Chromebook. It’s the cheapest way to get a premium experience.
  3. If you care about the screen quality for movies/editing: Get the ASUS Vivobook 16X. That display is unbeatable.
  4. If you game or edit video: Get the HP Victus 15 on sale.
  5. If you want the best long-term investment: Get the MacBook Air M1 refurbished.

Final Thoughts on Buying in 2024#

The biggest mistake you can make is buying a laptop from a brand you’ve never heard of just because it has “i7” in the title. Old i7s (like the 8th gen) are slower than modern i3s. Always check the generation of the processor.

Also, check the return policy. Budget laptops have a higher failure rate than premium ones. Buy from a retailer with a decent return window (Best Buy, Amazon, or Costco). Do not buy from a random third-party seller on eBay for these models.

One last tip: If you see a deal that seems too good to be true (like a $399 laptop with a touchscreen and 16GB RAM), read the reviews on the specific model number. Sometimes, manufacturers use the same model name but ship different internal parts to different retailers. The “Acer Aspire 5” sold at Walmart is sometimes different from the one at Amazon. Check the full spec sheet before hitting “Add to Cart.”

Happy hunting. The right budget laptop is out there, and it doesn’t have to cost a fortune.
