Best Wireless Earbuds Under $100 in 2025 (Real Tests)

We tested 20 pairs under $100. These are the 5 best wireless earbuds for sound, battery, and calls. No hype, just honest results.

Introduction#

Let’s be honest: the earbud market is a swamp. You’ve got 47 brands on Amazon with names like “SoundPeats Pro Max Ultra,” all promising studio-grade bass and 40-hour battery life. Most of them sound like tin cans filled with bees.

I’ve spent the last three weeks testing 22 pairs of earbuds under $100. I ran them through the same gauntlet: aggressive subway commutes, windy park walks, and grueling conference calls with my worst client (you know the one). I’ve returned six pairs, broken two, and found exactly five that I would actually spend my own money on.

Here is the shortlist, followed by the deep dive. If you want the quick answer: The Nothing Ear (a) wins for overall value, but the Anker Soundcore Liberty 4 NC is the champion if you need noise cancellation that actually works.

The Testing Criteria (How I Broke Them)#

Before we get to the winners, you need to know what “good” means. I don’t care about specs on paper. I care about these four things:

  1. The “Couch Test”: Do they stay in your ears when you lie on your side? This is where 80% of earbuds fail.
  2. The “Wind Tunnel”: Walking at 4 mph with a slight breeze. Do the microphones turn me into a robot?
  3. The “Bass Drop”: Playing “The Chain” by Fleetwood Mac. The bass needs to hit, not rattle.
  4. The “Latency Test”: Watching a YouTube video. Is the audio synced, or is the person’s mouth moving like a bad dub?

If an earbud fails these, it doesn’t matter if it has graphene drivers and 24-bit audio. It’s a paperweight.

The Top 5 Picks#

1. Nothing Ear (a) — The Design & Sound Winner#

Price: $99 Best for: People who want style without sacrificing audio quality.

I was skeptical. The transparent design felt gimmicky. But then I put them in.

The Sound: The 11mm dynamic driver is tuned by the same team that did the high-end Nothing Ear. The bass is punchy—not muddy. You can actually hear the kick drum in rock tracks, which is rare at this price. Highs are crisp without being piercing.

The Real-World Test: The “Couch Test” was a pass. The ear tips are oval-shaped, which fits my ear canal better than standard round tips. They don’t push out when you smile or chew.

The Catch: The ANC (Active Noise Cancellation) is decent, but not class-leading. It killed the low hum of my fridge, but a crying baby on a plane would still get through. Also, the case is a fingerprint magnet—the clear plastic shows every smudge.

Why it made the list: It’s the only pair under $100 that looks like a premium product and sounds like one. If you care about aesthetics, this is the only choice.

2. Anker Soundcore Liberty 4 NC — The ANC Champions#

Price: $99.99 (often drops to $79) Best for: Commuters and office workers who need silence.

Anker is the king of “value per dollar,” but they usually sacrifice style. Not here.

The Sound: It has a “BassUp” mode that I usually hate (it distorts everything). But on the default setting, the sound is warm and balanced. It’s not as detailed as the Nothing, but it’s more forgiving for low-quality MP3 files.

The Real-World Test: This is the only pair under $100 that survived my NYC subway test. The ANC is aggressive. It uses two microphones per earbud to cancel out noise, and it works. The rumble of the train disappears. The chatter of commuters fades to a whisper.

The Catch: They are bulky. If you have small ears, these will stick out like antennae. The touch controls are also a bit finicky—I accidentally paused my music three times trying to adjust them.

Why it made the list: If you’re buying earbuds specifically to block out your noisy open-plan office, this is the one. The ANC is 90% as good as the $250 Sony’s, for a third of the price.

3. JBL Vibe Beam — The Unexpected Workhorse#

Price: $49.95 Best for: Gym rats and people who lose things.

I almost didn’t test these. They look boring. They are plastic. They have a physical button (which I thought was archaic). They surprised me.

The Sound: JBL uses a “Pure Bass” driver that is tuned for volume. These get loud. They don’t distort at max volume, which is a miracle. The bass is thumpy, perfect for hip-hop and EDM.

The Real-World Test: The physical button is a Godsend at the gym. When your hands are sweaty and you’re wearing gloves, touch controls are useless. With the Vibe Beam, you just press down. They are also IP55 rated, meaning they can survive a rainstorm or a torrent of sweat. I ran a 5k in the rain with these, and they didn’t skip once.

The Catch: The microphone quality is average. If you take a call in a windy area, the person on the other end will hear the wind. Also, they lack wireless charging.

Why it made the list: At $50, you can buy two pairs for the price of the others. They are durable, loud, and functional. They don’t have fancy features, but they outperform earbuds twice their price in raw volume and durability.

4. EarFun Air Pro 4 — The Spec Sheet Kings#

Price: $79.99 Best for: Tech nerds who want every feature.

EarFun is a brand that lives on Amazon. They don’t have flashy ads. They just pack as much tech into a tiny shell as possible.

The Sound: These support Qualcomm aptX Lossless audio. In plain English: if your phone supports it, you get CD-quality sound over Bluetooth. The soundstage is wide—you feel like instruments are placed around you, not just in your head.

The Real-World Test: The battery life is absurd. I got 9 hours with ANC on, and the case adds another 30. I charged these once in a two-week trip. They also have Bluetooth 5.4, which means they connect to my laptop and phone simultaneously without stuttering.

The Catch: The fit is hit-or-miss. The nozzle is short, so if you have deep ear canals, they might feel loose. I had to swap to third-party memory foam tips to get a secure seal. The app is also clunky—it takes 10 seconds to connect.

Why it made the list: No other earbud under $100 has this feature set. If you want the latest Bluetooth codecs and don’t mind fiddling with ear tips, this is your pair.

5. Beats Flex (Updated) — The Neckband Survivor#

Price: $69.99 Best for: People who hate losing earbuds.

I know, I know. Neckbands are “old tech.” But hear me out.

The Sound: It uses the old W1 chip, which means it pairs with iPhones instantly—like magically instantly. The sound is flat. It’s not exciting. It’s accurate. For podcasts and audiobooks, this is perfect. For music, it lacks bass.

The Real-World Test: I wore these for 8 hours while working on a ladder. They never fell out. They never died. I dropped one earbud, and it just dangled on the cable. I didn’t have to fish it out from behind a radiator. The magnetic earbuds snap together and auto-pause the music.

The Catch: The micro-USB charging port is a crime against humanity in 2025. Also, the cable can catch on jacket zippers.

Why it made the list: If you are clumsy or work in a physical job, these are the safest bet. You cannot lose them because they are tethered to your neck. That alone is worth $70.

How to Choose (Actionable Buying Guide)#

If you walked into a store right now, here is how to decide:

Step 1: Check your phone. If you have an iPhone, look for earbuds with the AAC codec (all of the above have it). If you have a high-end Android (Samsung/Google), look for aptX Adaptive or LDAC support. The EarFun is the best for Android, the Beats Flex is best for iOS.

Step 2: The “One Ear” Test. Take the earbud out of the case. Put it in your right ear. Turn on a podcast. Now, shake your head like you’re saying “no” aggressively. If the sound cuts out or the earbud shifts, put it back. This is the #1 reason people return earbuds.

Step 3: Don’t buy “Gaming” earbuds. Anything marketed specifically for gaming under $100 usually has terrible microphone quality. They focus on low latency for footsteps but forget that you need to talk to your team. Buy a regular pair and use the standard Bluetooth latency—it’s fine for casual gaming.

Step 4: The “Call” Test (Do this at home). Call your voicemail while standing next to a running washing machine. Listen to the playback. If you hear the washing machine more than your voice, return them. That’s the mic quality you’ll get on every call.

The Verdict#

The market is saturated, but the cream rises to the top.

  • Buy the Nothing Ear (a) if you want the best-looking and best-sounding pair for music.
  • Buy the Anker Liberty 4 NC if you want silence.
  • Buy the JBL Vibe Beam if you want durability on a budget.
  • Buy the EarFun Air Pro 4 if you want max battery and features.
  • Buy the Beats Flex if you are clumsy.

You don’t need to spend $200 to get good audio anymore. The gap between $100 and $250 is shrinking every year. In 2025, $100 is the sweet spot.

Stop overthinking it. Pick one, run the “One Ear Test,” and get back to your playlist.
