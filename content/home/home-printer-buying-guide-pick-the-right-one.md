## Introduction

The last printer I bought cost me $89. Over three years, I spent $340 on ink. That's the dirty secret of the printer industry: the machine is cheap, the ink is not. If you're shopping for a home printer, the sticker price is the least important number on the box.

This guide walks you through what actually matters—cost per page, print volume, connection options, and the specific features worth paying for. By the end, you'll know exactly which type of printer fits your household, and you'll avoid the trap that catches most buyers.

## Start With One Question: What Will You Actually Print?

Before comparing specs, be honest about your habits. Printer buyers fall into three rough categories:

- **The occasional printer:** You print boarding passes, school forms, and the odd return label. Maybe 10–30 pages a month, mostly black and white.
- **The family workhorse:** Homework, recipes, coloring pages, tax documents, the occasional photo. 50–200 pages a month, mixed color and black.
- **The side-hustler or remote worker:** Contracts, shipping labels, presentations, maybe craft projects. 200+ pages a month, and reliability matters more than price.

Your category determines everything else. An occasional printer who buys a $400 color laser has wasted money. A side-hustler who buys a $60 inkjet will replace it within a year.

## Inkjet vs. Laser: The Real Trade-Off

### Inkjet printers

Inkjets spray microscopic droplets of liquid ink onto paper. They're cheap upfront, compact, and excellent for color—especially photos.

The catch: ink dries out. If you print twice a month, you'll spend more on cleaning cycles and replacement cartridges than on actual printing. Inkjets also tend to be slower and noisier.

**Best for:** households that print color regularly, photo enthusiasts, and anyone who needs a scanner/copier combo on a tight budget.

### Laser printers

Lasers fuse powdered toner onto paper with heat. They're faster, sharper on text, and—crucially—toner doesn't dry out. Leave a laser printer untouched for six months and it prints the first page perfectly.

The catch: higher upfront cost, and color lasers get expensive fast. Entry-level color laser toner sets can run $200+.

**Best for:** high-volume black-and-white printing, anyone who prints in bursts with long gaps in between, and home offices.

**Quick rule of thumb:** If you print fewer than 50 pages a month and mostly in black, a monochrome laser is almost always the smarter buy. If color matters, an inkjet with cheap third-party or refillable ink is the better path.

## The Number That Actually Matters: Cost Per Page

Here's how to calculate it in two minutes. Find the cartridge's page yield (printed on the box or the manufacturer's site) and divide the cartridge price by that number.

Example: A black ink cartridge costs $32 and yields 200 pages. That's **16 cents per page**. Print 100 pages a month and you're spending $16 monthly—$192 a year—on a printer you paid $70 for.

Now compare: a monochrome laser toner cartridge costs $75 and yields 1,200 pages. That's **6 cents per page**. Same volume, $72 a year.

### Watch for these cost traps

- **Starter cartridges:** Many new printers ship with "starter" cartridges that hold a fraction of a normal one. You'll be buying ink within weeks.
- **Tri-color cartridges:** If cyan, magenta, and yellow share one cartridge, replacing it because one color ran out wastes the other two. Look for individual color cartridges.
- **Subscription ink programs:** HP Instant Ink, Canon Pixma Print Plan, and similar services can be genuinely good deals—but only if your printing volume matches the tier. Overestimate and you're paying for pages you never print.

## Features Worth Paying For (and Ones That Aren't)

### Worth it

- **Automatic duplexing (double-sided printing):** Saves paper and makes documents look professional. Nearly essential for school and work printing.
- **Wi-Fi and mobile printing:** Printing from your phone via AirPrint, Google Cloud Print successor apps, or the manufacturer's app is genuinely convenient. Check that it works with your phone's OS before buying.
- **Flatbed scanner:** Even if you rarely scan, a flatbed beats a sheet-fed scanner for books, IDs, and odd-sized documents.
- **A real paper tray:** Top-loading trays that hold 100+ sheets beat rear-feed slots that hold 20.

### Skip it

- **Touchscreens:** A small color LCD is fine. A $50 premium for a touchscreen on a home printer is money better spent on ink.
- **Photo-lab quality claims:** Unless you're printing gallery prints, a standard inkjet on good paper is plenty. Dedicated photo printers are a niche purchase.
- **Fax:** You haven't needed one since 2009.

## Inkjet Refills and Third-Party Cartridges: The Honest Truth

Third-party cartridges can cut your cost per page by 50–70%. But there are real risks: some printers detect and reject them via firmware, print quality varies, and a bad cartridge can clog a printhead.

Two safer alternatives:

1. **Refillable ink tank printers** (Epson EcoTank, Canon MegaTank, Brother INKvestment Tank). You pour ink from bottles. Cost per page drops to under a cent for black. Upfront cost is higher ($200–$400), but if you print 100+ pages a month, it pays for itself within a year.
2. **Manufacturer subscription plans.** If your volume is predictable, these lock in low per-page costs and ship ink automatically.

If you go third-party, buy from a seller with a return policy and check your printer model's compatibility list first.

## Matching Printers to Real Households

**Small apartment, light printing:** A compact monochrome laser like the Brother HL-L2350DW. Around $120, 4–5 cents per page, Wi-Fi, duplexing. It'll last years.

**Family with kids:** An inkjet all-in-one with individual color cartridges and a flatbed scanner. Budget $100–$150 and factor in ink costs. Tank models are worth the stretch if you print coloring pages weekly.

**Remote worker or small business:** A color laser all-in-one or a tank inkjet. Prioritize duplexing, a 250-sheet tray, and Ethernet support. Expect $250–$500.

**Photo hobbyist:** A six-ink photo inkjet. Accept that you'll pay 30–50 cents per 4x6 print. It's the price of quality.

## Before You Check Out: A 60-Second Checklist

- Look up the cost per page for black and color cartridges.
- Confirm the printer supports your phone and computer operating systems.
- Check whether cartridges are individual or combined.
- Read reviews specifically about reliability after 6–12 months, not just unboxing impressions.
- Measure your shelf space. Printers are bigger than they look in photos.
- Check the return window. Printers are notorious for arriving with issues.

## The Bottom Line

Buy for the ink, not the printer. A $200 tank inkjet that costs a penny a page will beat a $60 bargain printer that eats $30 cartridges every six weeks—every single time. Figure out your monthly page volume, do the two-minute cost-per-page math, and pick the machine that fits your actual habits. Your future self, the one not standing in a store aisle at 9 p.m. before a school project is due, will thank you.
