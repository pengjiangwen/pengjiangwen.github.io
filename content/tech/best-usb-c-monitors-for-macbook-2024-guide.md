## What to Look for in a USB-C Monitor for MacBook

Before we get to the picks, you need to understand three critical specs that make or break a MacBook monitor experience.

### Power Delivery: 60W vs 96W

The USB-C port on the monitor must deliver enough wattage to charge your MacBook while it’s running. A MacBook Air (M2) draws about 30W under light use. A MacBook Pro 14” (M3 Pro) can pull 70W+ during video export. If your monitor only delivers 60W, a Pro model will slowly drain its battery under heavy load.

**Actionable tip:** If you own a 14” or 16” MacBook Pro, look for monitors with at least 85W USB-C power delivery. For Air models, 60W is sufficient.

### Panel Quality: IPS, VA, or OLED

MacBooks have exceptional displays—P3 wide color, high brightness, and excellent contrast. A cheap monitor makes your MacBook look like a toy. Aim for:
- **IPS panels** for accurate colors and wide viewing angles (best for most users)
- **OLED** for deep blacks and HDR (expensive, but stunning)
- **Avoid VA panels** for MacBooks unless you exclusively do static work—they have poor viewing angles that clash with macOS’s clean interface

### Daisy-Chaining and Hub Features

Not all USB-C monitors have a built-in USB hub. If you want to connect a webcam, microphone, or external drive, make sure the monitor has at least two downstream USB-A ports and maybe an SD card slot. Some monitors support daisy-chaining (connecting two monitors via DisplayPort out), which is a game-changer for multi-screen setups.

## Common Setup Issues (and How to Fix Them)

Even with the best monitor, MacBooks can be finicky. Here are three problems I’ve encountered and their solutions.

### Problem: Monitor not charging the MacBook

**Fix:** Check the USB-C cable. Not all USB-C cables support power delivery. Use the cable that came with the monitor or a certified 100W USB-C cable. Also, some monitors only deliver full power through a specific USB-C port (labeled “USB-C PD” or “Thunderbolt”).

### Problem: Blurry text on 4K monitors

**Fix:** macOS uses subpixel antialiasing that works best at certain resolutions. For 27” 4K monitors, run at 2560x1440 “Looks like” scaling. For 32” 4K, try 3008x1692. Go to System Settings > Displays > Scaled. If text still looks fuzzy, disable “Font smoothing” in Terminal with:
```
defaults -currentHost write -globalDomain AppleFontSmoothing -int 0
```

### Problem: Monitor wakes up slowly or doesn’t wake from sleep

**Fix:** This is a common macOS issue with third-party monitors. Update your Mac to the latest macOS version. If it persists, disable “Power Nap” in System Settings > Battery > Power Adapter. Also, make sure the monitor firmware is up to date (check the manufacturer’s support page).
