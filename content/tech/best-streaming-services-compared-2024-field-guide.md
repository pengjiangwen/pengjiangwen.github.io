## Introduction  

You know the feeling. It’s Friday night. You open Netflix, scroll for eleven minutes, close it. Open Hulu, scroll, close it. Open Max, scroll, and suddenly it’s 9:15 PM and you’ve watched nothing.  

The problem isn’t you. It’s that streaming services have become bloated, overlapping, and confusing. In 2024, there are over 300 streaming platforms in the US alone. The average household subscribes to four of them and pays roughly $87/month — which is more than a cable bill.  

I’ve tested every major service for the last six months, tracked their price hikes, counted actual new releases (not just library filler), and stress-tested their apps on smart TVs, phones, and web browsers. Here’s the honest, no-sponsorship breakdown of what’s worth your money right now.  

---

## The Big Three: Netflix, Hulu, and Max  

### Netflix – Still the Default, But Not the Best Value  

**Price:** $6.99 (ads), $15.49 (standard), $22.99 (premium)  
**Streams:** 1 (ads), 2 (standard), 4 (premium)  
**Killer Feature:** Originals library and global reach  

Netflix is the only service that feels like a cultural event. When *Stranger Things* drops, your group chat explodes. When *Squid Game* season 2 arrives, it’s unavoidable. That’s real value — but it comes with baggage.  

The ad tier is $6.99, but it’s capped at 720p and you can’t download shows. The standard tier at $15.49 doesn’t include 4K. To get 4K, you need the $22.99 premium plan, which is genuinely absurd.  

**The hidden gem:** Netflix’s international catalog. If you change your profile language to Spanish or Korean, you unlock regional titles that never hit the US homepage. I found a brilliant Argentine thriller called *El Reino* that would never surface otherwise.  

**Verdict:** Keep it if you watch at least three original series per year. Otherwise, skip it and subscribe for one month when something big drops.

### Hulu – The Underrated Workhorse  

**Price:** $7.99 (ads), $17.99 (no ads), $9.99 (with Disney+ bundle)  
**Streams:** 2 (both tiers)  
**Killer Feature:** Next-day broadcast TV and FX shows  

Hulu doesn’t get the hype, but it’s the best utility player. You get next-day episodes of ABC, NBC, and Fox shows. More importantly, Hulu is the exclusive home of FX content — *The Bear*, *Shōgun*, *Reservation Dogs* — which is arguably the highest quality TV being made right now.  

The ad tier is $7.99, and honestly, the ads aren’t bad. You get about 90 seconds per break, and Hulu doesn’t repeat the same commercial five times in a row (looking at you, Peacock).  

**The hidden gem:** The Disney+ bundle. For $9.99 with ads, you get Hulu, Disney+, and ESPN+. That’s three services for less than the cost of one Netflix premium tier. If you have kids or watch any sports, this is the single best deal in streaming.  

**Verdict:** If you watch network TV dramas or FX originals, Hulu is non-negotiable. The bundle makes it a no-brainer.

### Max – The Quality Over Quantity Champion  

**Price:** $9.99 (ads), $16.99 (no ads), $20.99 (4K)  
**Streams:** 2 (all tiers)  
**Killer Feature:** HBO originals and Warner Bros. film library  

Max (formerly HBO Max) has the highest density of "must-watch" content per dollar. *The Last of Us*, *House of the Dragon*, *Succession*, *The Wire*, *Sopranos* — plus every Christopher Nolan film, every Studio Ghibli movie, and the full DC catalog.  

The base ad tier is $9.99, but here’s the catch: Max’s ad tier doesn’t include HBO shows in 4K, and some movies are randomly missing (licensing quirks). The $16.99 no-ads tier is the sweet spot.  

**The hidden gem:** The TCM hub. Max has a curated collection of classic films that rotates monthly. I watched *Casablanca* and *12 Angry Men* on a rainy Sunday — you won’t find that depth on Netflix.  

**Verdict:** The best choice for movie lovers and prestige TV fans. If you only subscribe to one service, this is it.

## The Niche Powerhouses: Disney+, Apple TV+, and Peacock  

### Disney+ – Family Value, But Not for Adults  

**Price:** $7.99 (ads), $13.99 (no ads), $9.99 (bundle)  
**Streams:** 4 (all tiers)  
**Killer Feature:** Simultaneous streams and entire Disney vault  

Disney+ allows four simultaneous streams on the base plan. That’s huge for families. Netflix charges you $22.99 for that privilege.  

But for adults without kids, Disney+ is thin. The Marvel and Star Wars output has slowed considerably. In the last six months, I counted exactly three new Marvel series and one Star Wars show. That’s not enough to justify a monthly subscription.  

**The hidden gem:** National Geographic content. *Cosmos* and *Welcome to Earth* are stunning in 4K HDR.  

**Verdict:** Essential if you have kids. Optional if you don’t. Get the bundle with Hulu if you want both.

### Apple TV+ – Small Catalog, Home Run Quality  

**Price:** $9.99 (no ads, no tiers)  
**Streams:** 6 (yes, six)  
**Killer Feature:** 4K HDR on everything, no ads ever, six family streams  

Apple TV+ has the smallest catalog of any major service — about 250 titles total. But the hit rate is staggering. *Severance*, *Ted Lasso*, *Slow Horses*, *Killers of the Flower Moon*, *CODA*. They don’t make filler.  

The pricing is refreshingly simple: $9.99, no ad tier, no 4K upcharge. And you get six simultaneous streams, which is overkill but nice.  

**The hidden gem:** Apple TV+ is free for three months if you buy any new Apple device. Also, many credit cards (like the Apple Card) offer periodic free periods.  

**Verdict:** The best "second service" to pair with Hulu or Max. Small but elite.

### Peacock – The Sports and Reality Sleeper  

**Price:** $5.99 (ads), $11.99 (no ads)  
**Streams:** 3 (both tiers)  
**Killer Feature:** Live sports (Premier League, WWE) and *The Office*  

Peacock is the most underrated service, mostly because its interface is clunky and its originals are forgettable. But for sports fans, it’s essential. Premier League soccer, WWE pay-per-views, and Olympic coverage are all exclusive to Peacock.  

The $5.99 ad tier is fine, but the ads are repetitive. You’ll see the same insurance commercial six times per episode.  

**The hidden gem:** The full *Law & Order* and *Chicago* franchise library. If you like procedural dramas, this is a goldmine.  

**Verdict:** Worth it for sports fans and sitcom comfort-watchers. Otherwise, skip.

## The Dark Horses: Paramount+, Prime Video, and Tubi  

### Paramount+ – The CBS Back Catalog  

**Price:** $5.99 (ads), $11.99 (no ads)  
**Streams:** 3  
**Killer Feature:** *Yellowstone* universe and live CBS  

Paramount+ is the bargain bin with a few diamonds. *Yellowstone* and its spinoffs are exclusive. So is *Star Trek* (all series) and *Tulsa King*. If you like those, it’s a steal at $5.99.  

**Verdict:** Niche appeal. Skip unless you’re a Trek or Sheridan fan.

### Prime Video – The Bonus That’s Actually Good  

**Price:** Included with Amazon Prime ($14.99/month or $139/year)  
**Streams:** 3  
**Killer Feature:** *The Boys*, *Reacher*, and free two-day shipping  

Prime Video is a bonus, not a primary service. But don’t sleep on it. *Reacher* season 2 was the most-watched streaming show of early 2024. *The Boys* is still elite. And you can rent new releases at reasonable prices.  

**Verdict:** If you already have Amazon Prime, use it. Don’t subscribe just for video.

### Tubi – The Free Champion  

**Price:** $0  
**Streams:** Unlimited  
**Killer Feature:** 50,000+ movies with ads  

Tubi is free, legal, and surprisingly good. It has a massive library of older films, B-movie gems, and a surprisingly deep collection of international cinema. The ads are frequent (about four minutes per 20-minute block), but for $0, it’s unbeatable.  

**Verdict:** The perfect filler for when you’ve exhausted your paid services.

## Actionable Tips: How to Optimize Your Streaming Setup  

### 1. Rotate, Don’t Stack  

Don’t subscribe to five services at once. Pick one primary (Max or Hulu), one secondary (Apple TV+ or Peacock), and rotate the rest monthly. It’s easy to cancel and resubscribe — most services keep your watch history for 6-12 months.

### 2. Use the "Cancel at the Door" Trick  

When you try to cancel, every service offers a discount. Netflix offers 30% off for three months. Hulu offers $2/month for six months. Max offers 50% off for two months. Always click through the cancellation flow to see the offer. I’ve never actually canceled a service without getting a deal.

### 3. Check Your Credit Card Benefits  

Amex Platinum gives you $240/year in streaming credits. Chase Sapphire Reserve gives $120/year. The Wells Fargo Autograph gives 3% cash back on streaming. If you’re paying full price, you’re leaving money on the table.

### 4. Use JustWatch to Track Availability  

Before subscribing to anything, check JustWatch.com. It tells you which service has which movie or show, and it tracks price changes. It saves me at least $15/month.

## The Final Verdict: What Should You Actually Subscribe To?

- **If you only pick one:** Max. Best overall library, best movies, best originals.
- **If you pick two:** Max + Hulu (with Disney+ bundle). You cover prestige TV, network TV, and family content.
- **If you pick three:** Max + Hulu bundle + Apple TV+. You have elite quality, broad coverage, and zero ad fatigue.
- **If you’re on a budget:** Hulu with ads ($7.99) + Tubi (free). That’s $8/month for more content than you can watch.
- **If you’re a sports fan:** Peacock + Hulu bundle. You get Premier League, WWE, and every FX show.

Streaming isn’t about having everything. It’s about having the right things at the right time. Rotate your subscriptions, use the cancellation discounts, and you’ll save $40/month while watching better content than 90% of people.

Now go cancel that Netflix premium tier you’re not using. You know you’re not watching 4K.
