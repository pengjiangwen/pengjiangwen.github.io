## Introduction

Let’s be honest: most of us use maybe 20% of what the iPhone can actually do. The other 80% is buried in menus, hidden behind long presses, or simply never advertised. I’ve been using iPhones since the 3GS, and I still stumble on features that make me wonder how I lived without them.

Last week, a friend watched me scan a document with the Notes app in about four seconds. She thought I was using a third-party scanner. She wasn’t. That’s the kind of gap I want to close for you.

These aren’t the “turn on dark mode” tips you’ve seen a hundred times. These are real, time-saving, battery-extending, workflow-changing tricks that work on iOS 17 and 18. Grab your iPhone and follow along.

## Master the Back Tap for Instant Actions

The back of your iPhone is a button you’ve never pressed. It’s called Back Tap, and it’s been hiding in Accessibility settings since iOS 14.

Go to **Settings > Accessibility > Touch > Back Tap**. You’ll see two options: Double Tap and Triple Tap. Assign an action to each. I use Double Tap to open the Control Center and Triple Tap to take a screenshot. But you can assign almost anything: open an app, mute your phone, launch the flashlight, or trigger a Shortcut.

**Real example:** I set Triple Tap to open the Magnifier app. When I’m reading tiny restaurant menus in dim light, two quick knocks on the back of the phone and I have a zoomed, brightened view. No fumbling for buttons.

The catch? It works best with a case that doesn’t dampen the tap sensitivity. Thin silicone or plastic cases are fine; thick OtterBox-style cases might require a firmer knock.

## The Hidden Trackpad on Your Keyboard

You’ve probably tried to move the cursor by poking at the text. It’s imprecise and frustrating. Here’s the fix: press and hold the space bar until the keyboard turns blank and gray. Now slide your finger anywhere on the keyboard area—the cursor moves exactly where you guide it.

This works in any text field: Messages, Notes, Mail, Safari. Once you get used to it, you’ll never go back to the old way.

**Pro tip:** If you have a 3D Touch-capable iPhone (iPhone 6s through XS), you can also force-press anywhere on the keyboard to activate this. On newer models without 3D Touch, only the space bar works. On iPads, you can use two fingers on the keyboard to slide the cursor.

## Scan Documents Instantly with the Notes App

Don’t download a separate scanning app. The Notes app has a built-in scanner that’s faster and cleaner than most dedicated tools.

Open a new note, tap the camera icon, and select **Scan Documents**. Hold your phone over the document—it auto-detects the edges and captures the image. You can adjust corners afterward, apply filters (Color, Grayscale, Black & White, Photo), and save as a PDF.

**Why this matters:** I used to use Adobe Scan. It’s good, but it takes longer to open, syncs to a cloud I don’t always want, and shows ads. The Notes scanner is instant, offline, and stores the PDF directly in the note. You can share it via AirDrop, Mail, or any messaging app right from there.

## Use the Hidden Magnifier as a Reading Aid

Your iPhone has a built-in magnifying glass that’s much more than a zoom. It’s actually a full-on digital loupe with contrast, brightness, and color filters.

Find it by triple-clicking the side button (or home button on older models). If it doesn’t open, go to **Settings > Accessibility > Magnifier** and toggle it on. Once active, you can:

- Adjust zoom level with a slider
- Change brightness and contrast
- Apply color filters (useful for color blindness or low-light reading)
- Freeze the frame (tap the shutter button) so you can read a tiny label without holding your phone steady

I use this constantly for reading serial numbers on electronics, ingredients on food packaging, and fine print on contracts. It’s also a lifesaver for older family members who refuse to carry reading glasses.

## Create Custom Vibration Patterns for Contacts

You can assign a unique vibration pattern to each contact. This way, you know who’s calling without looking at the screen.

Go to **Contacts > Select a person > Ringtone > Vibration > Create New Vibration**. Tap out a rhythm on the screen—short taps, long presses, pauses—and save it.

**Practical use:** I gave my wife a pattern of two short taps and a long one. When my phone buzzes in my pocket during a meeting, I know it’s her without pulling it out. I gave my boss a single short tap (so I know I can ignore it if I’m busy). My mom gets a frantic staccato pattern because, well, she’s my mom.

You can do the same for text tones under **Sounds & Haptics**.

## The Secret to One-Handed Keyboard Typing

If you’re using a Pro Max or Plus model, reaching the opposite side of the keyboard with one hand is a stretch. Apple built a one-handed keyboard mode years ago, and almost no one uses it.

Long-press the emoji or globe icon on the keyboard (bottom left). You’ll see three options: left-aligned, centered, and right-aligned keyboard. Tap the left or right alignment depending on which hand you’re using. The keyboard shrinks and shifts to that side.

To go back, tap the empty space on the opposite side of the keyboard.

**Why you’ll love this:** I type with one hand while holding a coffee cup or carrying groceries. The shifted keyboard puts the most common keys right under my thumb. It’s not perfect for fast typing, but it’s way better than stretching or dropping your phone.

## Batch Delete Photos Like a Pro

Deleting photos one by one is painful. The Photos app has a batch selection trick that speeds this up.

Open your photo library, tap **Select** (top right), then drag your finger across the photos you want to delete. You don’t have to tap each one. Just swipe in a row. For multiple rows, keep your finger down and zigzag.

**Advanced trick:** To select non-adjacent photos, start dragging to select the first batch, then use a second finger to tap individual photos outside the selection range. This works on iPad too.

Once selected, tap the trash icon. Don’t forget to go to **Recently Deleted** and permanently clear them if you need the storage back immediately.

## Use Siri Shortcuts for Repetitive Tasks

Siri Shortcuts are powerful, but most people ignore them because they seem complicated. Let me simplify: think of a task you do every day that takes three or more steps. That’s a candidate for a Shortcut.

**Example:** Every morning, I check the weather, turn on my smart lights, and start a 10-minute meditation timer. That’s three separate apps. I created one Shortcut called “Morning Mode” that does all three in sequence. I trigger it by saying, “Hey Siri, Morning Mode.”

To create one:
1. Open the Shortcuts app (it’s pre-installed, don’t delete it).
2. Tap the **+** icon.
3. Tap **Add Action** and search for the first app or action (e.g., Weather > Get Current Weather).
4. Tap the **+** again to add the next action.
5. Continue until your sequence is complete.
6. Name the Shortcut and tap **Done**.

You can also add Shortcuts to your Home Screen as a button. I have one for “Driving to Work” that opens Apple Maps, starts a podcast, and sends my ETA to my wife. It saves me about 45 seconds every morning.

## The Calculator Backspace Swipe

If you type a wrong digit in the Calculator app, don’t tap C and start over. Swipe left or right on the number display. Each swipe deletes the last digit you entered.

This is one of those small features that feels obvious once you know it, but it’s completely invisible. I’ve watched people frantically tap C and re-enter long numbers. One swipe saves the day.

**Bonus:** Turn your phone sideways to reveal the scientific calculator. It includes logarithms, exponents, and trigonometric functions.

## Instantly Share Wi-Fi Passwords Without Typing

You’re at a friend’s house, and they ask for the Wi-Fi password. Instead of reading a long, case-sensitive string, use Apple’s built-in password sharing.

Make sure both devices have Bluetooth and Wi-Fi turned on. The person trying to connect selects your network. You get a pop-up on your phone that says “Share Wi-Fi Password.” Tap **Share Password**. Done.

**Requirements:** Both devices must be Apple (iPhone, iPad, or Mac). Both must have each other in their Contacts. If you’re not in their Contacts, it won’t work. In that case, just add each other quickly, or use the old-fashioned method.

This works in reverse too. If you’re the guest, just ask the host to tap their phone when you try to connect.

## Use AirDrop with Everyone for 10 Minutes

AirDrop is great, but the default setting (Contacts Only) means you can’t receive files from strangers. If you’re at a conference, party, or family gathering, you can temporarily open AirDrop to everyone.

Go to **Control Center > long-press the network card (top left with Wi-Fi/Bluetooth) > tap AirDrop > select Everyone for 10 Minutes**. This option was added in iOS 17. It automatically reverts to Contacts Only after 10 minutes, so you don’t accidentally leave it open.

**Why this matters:** I used to hand my phone to someone so they could AirDrop me a photo. That’s risky. Now they just tap my name from their phone. It’s faster and safer.

## The Best Camera Trick: Lock Exposure and Focus

You probably know you can tap to focus. But did you know you can lock both focus and exposure?

Open the Camera app, frame your shot, then tap and hold the subject on the screen until you see “AE/AF Lock” at the top. Now, even if you move the phone or the subject moves slightly, the focus and exposure stay fixed.

**Real scenario:** You’re filming a cooking video. You want the pan in focus, but your hand moves in and out of frame. Without AE/AF Lock, the camera keeps refocusing on your hand. With it locked, the pan stays sharp and the exposure stays consistent.

To unlock, tap anywhere on the screen.

## Use VoiceOver to Read Text Aloud

This one is technically an accessibility feature, but it’s useful for everyone. You can make your iPhone read any text on the screen aloud.

Go to **Settings > Accessibility > Spoken Content > Speak Screen**. Enable it. Now, swipe down with two fingers from the top of any screen, and Siri will start reading the text. A control panel appears where you can adjust speed, pause, or skip forward.

**Use cases:** Listen to a long article while driving (pull over first), have a recipe read to you while your hands are dirty, or proofread your own writing by hearing it spoken back. I use this to catch awkward phrasing in emails.

## Find Your AirPods with Precision Finding

If you lose an AirPod in the couch cushions, the regular Find My app shows you a map. That’s not helpful when it’s three feet away. If you have AirPods Pro (2nd generation) or AirPods Max, you can use Precision Finding.

Open the Find My app, select your AirPods, and tap **Find Nearby**. Your iPhone will guide you with an arrow, distance indicator, and haptic feedback. It uses the same U1 chip that helps you find your keys with an AirTag.

**Note:** This only works with AirPods that have the U1 chip. Older AirPods just show a map. If you’re in the market for new ones, the Pro 2nd gen is worth it for this feature alone.

## The Ultimate Battery Health Shortcut

You don’t need to dig into Settings to check battery health. Create a simple Shortcut that shows your battery percentage and health in one tap.

Open Shortcuts, create a new one, and add the action **Get Battery Level**. Then add **Get Device Details** and set it to **Battery Health**. Finally, add **Show Result**. Name it “Battery Check.” Add it to your Home Screen.

Now, one tap shows you both numbers. I check mine every two weeks to see if I need a battery replacement before it drops below 80%.

The iPhone is a tool that rewards curiosity. Every one of these tips took me less than five minutes to set up, but they’ve saved me hours over the years. The back tap alone probably saves me five seconds every time I need a screenshot—and I take a lot of screenshots.

Start with one or two that solve a real pain point. For me, it was the cursor control and the document scanner. For you, it might be the custom vibrations or the Wi-Fi sharing. Whatever you pick, the goal is the same: make your phone work for you, not the other way around.
