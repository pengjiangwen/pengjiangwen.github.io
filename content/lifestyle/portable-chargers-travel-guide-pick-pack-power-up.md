## Why Your Old Power Bank Is Lying to You

Before we talk about what to buy, let's talk about why your current one probably sucks. The biggest scam in the portable charger world is the **mAh (milliampere-hour)** label. That number tells you the capacity of the battery inside, but it doesn't tell you how much of that juice actually reaches your phone.

Here’s the dirty secret: energy is lost as heat. Converting the battery’s voltage (usually 3.7V) to your phone’s charging voltage (5V) is an inefficient process. On average, you lose about 20-30% of the total capacity just in that conversion.

**The Real-World Math:**
If you buy a 10,000 mAh power bank, you’re not getting 10,000 mAh into your iPhone or Samsung. You’re getting roughly 6,500 to 7,500 mAh.

- **iPhone 15 Pro:** ~3,200 mAh battery
- **Samsung S24 Ultra:** ~5,000 mAh battery

So, that 10,000 mAh bank you bought thinking it would charge your phone three times? It’ll actually only do it twice—maybe less if you’re using your phone heavily while charging.

**The Takeaway:** Don't buy a 5,000 mAh bank thinking it's a "day saver." It's an "emergency top-off." For travel, your baseline should be **10,000 mAh** if you’re a light user, and **20,000 mAh** if you’re a power user or traveling with a tablet.

## The 3 Types of Travel Chargers (And Which You Need)

You don't need to carry a car battery in your backpack. There are three distinct categories, and you likely only need one of them—maybe two.

### 1. The Pocket Rocket (5,000 - 10,000 mAh)
**Best for:** City breaks, nights out, and minimalists.
These are the slim, lightweight banks that slip into a jean pocket or a small crossbody bag. They usually have a single USB-C port and are perfect for giving your phone that one extra full charge to get you through a late dinner and a cab ride home.

**My Pick:** Look for one with a built-in USB-C cable. It sounds like a small detail, but it saves you from the "I forgot my cable at the hostel" crisis. It’s one less thing to carry and one less thing to lose.

### 2. The Workhorse (20,000 mAh)
**Best for:** Long-haul flights, road trips, and content creators.
This is the sweet spot for 90% of travelers. It’s heavy enough to charge a phone four or five times, or a tablet once. It’s the bank that lets you land in a new country with a 100% phone, even after a 10-hour flight where you watched movies the entire way.

**Crucial Feature:** This size *must* have **Power Delivery (PD)** support. PD is a fast-charging protocol that lets the bank output 20W-45W of power. This is what allows your phone to charge from 0 to 50% in 30 minutes instead of 3 hours. Without PD, a 20,000 mAh bank takes forever to charge itself and your devices.

### 3. The Beast (25,000 mAh +)
**Best for:** Camping, vanlife, or traveling with a laptop.
These are the heavyweights. They can charge a MacBook, a drone, and a camera battery all at once. They are usually too heavy for daily sightseeing, so they live in a daypack or a car.

**Warning:** If you fly frequently, be careful. **Airlines restrict batteries over 100Wh (Watt-hours)** in carry-on luggage. Most 20,000 mAh banks are under this limit (around 74Wh), but some 30,000 mAh banks are over. Always check the Wh rating on the label before you book that budget airline ticket.

## Packing Smart: The "Charging Kit" Philosophy

Buying a great power bank is only half the battle. The other half is how you pack it. I used to throw my charger loose in my bag, and it was a disaster—scratched ports, tangled cables, and a dead battery when I needed it most. Here’s my streamlined kit:

**The Cable Equation:**
- **Short and Fast:** Carry a 1-foot USB-C to USB-C cable for the power bank. It reduces clutter and charges faster than a long cable.
- **The Adapter:** Buy a small USB-C to Lightning (if you have an iPhone) or USB-C to USB-C adapter. This lets you use your main cable for everything.

**The "To-Go" Pouch:**
Don't just throw this stuff in your bag. Use a small tech pouch—even a pencil case works. In it, I keep:
1.  The power bank.
2.  A 30W wall charger (this is key—it charges the power bank itself twice as fast as a standard 5W brick).
3.  The short cable.
4.  A set of earbuds.

**Pro-Tip:** Charge your power bank the night before you leave. A 20,000 mAh bank takes 4-5 hours to fill with a fast wall charger. Don't try to do it at the airport gate.

## Real-World Charging Strategies (Don't Be That Person)

Having the gear is one thing. Knowing how to use it efficiently is another. Here are the strategies I use on every trip now.

### 1. The "Top-Up" Method
Don't wait until your phone hits 5%. That’s when the battery is most stressed and charges the slowest. Instead, plug in when you hit 40-50%. This keeps your phone in the "sweet spot" of battery health and uses less power from your bank to get back to 100%.

### 2. Airplane Mode is Your Friend
If you’re in a dead zone or on a plane, put your phone in Airplane Mode *while* it’s plugged into the bank. Your phone won't be constantly searching for a signal (which is a massive battery drain), so it will charge nearly 30% faster.

### 3. Charge Your Bank, Not Your Phone, at the Airport
You’re sitting at the gate with a wall outlet right next to you. Don't plug your phone into the wall. Plug your **power bank** into the wall. Now you have a free, full bank for the plane, and you can use your phone freely. This is the ultimate power move.

## The Final Checklist

Before you zip your bag, run through this checklist to make sure you’re covered:

- **Capacity:** Is it at least 10,000 mAh? (Yes for day trips, 20,000 for long hauls).
- **Speed:** Does it support Power Delivery (PD) or Quick Charge (QC)? (If not, it’s a paperweight).
- **Cables:** Do you have the right cable for your phone *and* your bank?
- **Wall Brick:** Is your wall charger fast enough to refill the bank overnight?
- **Airline Check:** Is the Wh rating under 100? (Look at the small print on the side).
