Best Credit Cards for Travel Rewards in 2024

Compare the best travel rewards credit cards for 2024. Learn how to earn points, score free flights, and pick the right card for your spending habits.
September 17, 2026 · 5 min · 1060 words · TechLife Guide

Introduction#

Two years ago, my friend Dana spent $4,200 on a two-week trip to Portugal. Flights, hotels, trains, the works. She paid for exactly none of it with cash — it all came from points she’d accumulated over 18 months of normal spending on groceries, gas, and a few work dinners.

She didn’t do anything exotic. No manufactured spending, no shady tricks. She just picked the right card, put her regular expenses on it, and let the signup bonus do the heavy lifting.

That’s the thing about travel rewards cards: the difference between a mediocre card and a great one isn’t a few dollars. It’s often $1,000 or more in value per year. But the “best” card depends entirely on how you spend, where you want to go, and whether you’re willing to juggle more than one card.

Here’s how to figure out which one actually fits your life.

Start With Your Travel Goal, Not the Card#

Most people do this backward. They sign up for a card because it has a shiny 100,000-point bonus, then scramble to find a way to use the points.

Flip the order:

  • Want a free flight to Europe? You need a card that transfers to airline partners like Air France/KLM Flying Blue or Virgin Atlantic.
  • Prefer cash back you can spend on anything? A flat-rate card beats a points card every time.
  • Fly Southwest or JetBlue domestically? Their co-branded cards often beat transferable points for that specific use case.
  • Stay at Hyatt or Marriott regularly? Hotel cards can deliver 10x+ value on those stays.

Write down your next two trips before you apply for anything. It takes five minutes and saves you from a wasted hard inquiry.

The Three Categories of Travel Cards#

1. Transferable Points Cards#

These are the Swiss Army knives of travel rewards. You earn points that transfer to multiple airlines and hotels, usually at a 1:1 ratio.

Top options:

  • Chase Sapphire Preferred — $95 annual fee, 60,000-point signup bonus (worth roughly $750 in travel), transfers to United, Southwest, Hyatt, and more. Great starter card.
  • Chase Sapphire Reserve — $550 annual fee, but includes a $300 travel credit, Priority Pass lounge access, and 3x on dining and travel. Worth it if you travel more than four times a year.
  • Amex Platinum — $695 annual fee, enormous lounge network, $200 airline credit, $200 Uber credit, $240 digital entertainment credit. Only makes sense if you’ll actually use the perks.
  • Capital One Venture X — $395 annual fee, $300 travel credit, 10,000 anniversary miles, lounge access. The math works out to nearly free if you travel once a year.

2. Airline Co-Branded Cards#

These earn miles directly in one airline’s program. The value is narrower but often deeper — free checked bags, priority boarding, and companion passes can outweigh the points.

Worth considering:

  • Southwest Rapid Rewards Priority — $149 fee, 7,500 anniversary points, $75 Southwest credit, and the companion pass potential if you fly often.
  • United Explorer — $95 fee (waived first year), free checked bag, two lounge passes per year.
  • Alaska Airlines Visa Signature — $95 fee, annual companion fare from $122, free checked bag.

Skip these if you don’t fly that airline at least twice a year. The perks won’t pay for the fee.

3. Hotel Cards#

  • World of Hyatt Credit Card — $95 fee, annual free night certificate worth up to 15,000 points. If you stay at Hyatt once a year, this card pays for itself.
  • Marriott Bonvoy Boundless — $95 fee, annual free night up to 35,000 points, 15 elite night credits.
  • IHG One Rewards Premier — $99 fee, annual free night, fourth night free on award stays.

Hotel cards are the most underrated category. A single free night at a $300 hotel covers three years of annual fees.

The Signup Bonus Math Nobody Talks About#

Here’s a real example. Chase Sapphire Preferred currently offers 60,000 points after spending $4,000 in three months. Those points transfer to Hyatt at 1:1, and Hyatt Category 4 hotels cost 15,000 points per night.

That’s four free nights. At a $250 nightly rate, you’re looking at $1,000 in value from one signup bonus — minus the $95 annual fee.

But there’s a catch: you have to hit the minimum spend organically. Don’t buy things you don’t need just to hit a bonus. If your normal spending is $1,500 a month, a $4,000 minimum is easy. If it’s $800, pick a card with a lower threshold.

How to Actually Maximize Your Points#

Stack categories. Use a 3x dining card for restaurants and a 2x card for everything else. Two cards beat one almost every time.

Don’t hoard points. Airlines devalue programs every few years. Points sitting in an account lose value. Use them.

Transfer, don’t redeem through the portal. Chase points are worth 1.25 cents through their travel portal, but 2+ cents when transferred to Hyatt. The difference is massive.

Watch for transfer bonuses. Amex and Chase regularly run 20-30% transfer bonuses to specific partners. Timing a transfer during a bonus can add hundreds of dollars in value.

Book early, but not too early. Award seats open roughly 330 days out for most airlines. The best availability is usually 2-6 months before departure.

Which Card Should You Actually Get?#

If you’re new to travel rewards: Chase Sapphire Preferred. Low fee, strong bonus, flexible points.

If you travel frequently and want lounges: Capital One Venture X. Best value premium card right now.

If you fly one airline constantly: Get that airline’s co-branded card.

If you want simple cash back: Chase Freedom Unlimited or Citi Double Cash. No annual fee, no complexity.

If you stay at Hyatt or Marriott regularly: Add a hotel card to your wallet.

A Final Reality Check#

Travel rewards aren’t free money. They’re a rebate on spending you’d do anyway. If you carry a balance, the interest will wipe out every point you earn — pay in full every month or skip this entire category.

But if you pay your balance in full and you travel at least once a year, the right card can turn a $3,000 vacation into a $500 one. That’s not a gimmick. It’s just math, applied consistently over a few years.

Pick one card. Use it for everything. Hit the bonus. Book the trip. Then come back and add a second card once you’ve got the rhythm down.
